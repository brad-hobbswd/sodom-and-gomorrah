CONCLUSION

WHY SODOM STILL MATTERS

The story of Sodom has survived for thousands of years because every generation has found something in it that speaks to its own circumstances.

For ancient Israel, Sodom became a warning about rebellion against God.

For the prophets, it became a picture of social corruption and injustice.

For the writers of the New Testament, it became an example of judgment and moral rebellion.

For later generations, Sodom became increasingly connected with debates about sexuality.

That later history is important, but it should not cause us to forget the complexity of the original biblical material.

The historical record does not give us permission to reduce Sodom to one modern word.

Nor does it allow us to erase the sexual elements from Genesis 19, Jude, or the laws of Leviticus.

The story contains all of these themes.

There is pride.

There is wealth without compassion.

There is neglect of the vulnerable.

There is violence.

There is hostility toward strangers.

There is sexual violence.

There is rebellion against God.

There is judgment.

And there is a continuing question about what happens when a society becomes so comfortable with its own power that it no longer recognizes the needs of the people around it.

That is why the history of Sodom is larger than an argument about sexuality.

It is the history of how an ancient story became one of the most powerful moral symbols in the biblical tradition.

And before we decide what Sodom means for our own time, we should first understand what Sodom meant to the people who told the story, preserved it, interpreted it, and passed it from one generation to another.
