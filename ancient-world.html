PART ONE

THE ANCIENT WORLD

CHAPTER 1

Before Sodom: The World of Abraham

Before we can understand Sodom, we need to understand the world in which the story takes place.

The world described in Genesis was very different from the modern world.

There were no modern governments, police departments, hospitals, social welfare systems, or international organizations. Communities depended heavily upon family relationships, tribal connections, alliances, and local rulers.

Travel could be dangerous.

A person arriving in a strange city could be vulnerable because they did not have family nearby to protect them.

This is why hospitality was so important throughout the ancient Near East.

Welcoming a traveler was not simply a matter of being polite. Food, shelter, protection, and family honor could all be connected to the treatment of strangers.

This background becomes extremely important when we eventually arrive at Genesis 19.

CHAPTER 2

Sodom Before the Destruction

Genesis introduces Sodom before its destruction.

Genesis 13:10 describes the region around Sodom as well watered, like the garden of the LORD.

This detail is important.

Sodom was not initially presented as a barren wasteland.

It was located in a region associated with fertility and prosperity.

Lot chose the area because it appeared to offer opportunity.

Then Genesis 13:13 gives the first major warning:

The people of Sodom were wicked and were sinning greatly against the LORD.

The text does not yet give us a complete explanation of their wickedness.

That is historically significant.

The biblical story begins by telling us that something was wrong with the society before it later shows us specific examples of that corruption.

CHAPTER 3

The Outcry Against Sodom

Genesis 18 takes the story further.

God tells Abraham that the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is great and that their sin is very serious.

The language of an “outcry” is important in the ancient biblical world.

Throughout the Hebrew Bible, cries for help can be associated with people suffering under oppression, violence, injustice, or wrongdoing.

The text therefore presents Sodom as a place where something terrible had been happening for long enough that an outcry had reached God.

The story is moving toward judgment.

But the reader still has not been given a complete list of Sodom’s sins.

That comes through the story itself and through later biblical writers.
