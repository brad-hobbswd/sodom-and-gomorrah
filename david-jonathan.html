PART SEVEN

DAVID AND JONATHAN

The story of David and Jonathan comes from a completely different period and belongs to a different subject within the biblical tradition. Their story is not a story about Sodom. It is included here because it provides an important example of how the Bible describes an exceptionally deep relationship between two men.

Examining this relationship helps us maintain the same historical discipline used throughout this study. We can take the language of the text seriously without claiming that the text says more than it actually says.

CHAPTER 14

A Different Kind of Biblical Relationship

David lived during the establishment of Israel's monarchy, while Jonathan was the son of King Saul. Their relationship became one of the most emotionally powerful friendships described in the Hebrew Bible.

First Samuel 18 says that Jonathan's soul was bound to David's soul and that Jonathan loved David as himself. They made a covenant, Jonathan protected David, and David loved Jonathan deeply.

Their relationship involved extraordinary loyalty during a time of political danger. Saul increasingly viewed David as a threat, and Jonathan repeatedly placed his relationship with David above his own political interests and personal safety.

Jonathan warned David about danger and defended him before his father. Their relationship was therefore not simply emotional. It involved concrete acts of loyalty, protection, sacrifice, and covenant commitment.

When Jonathan died, David mourned him deeply.

In 2 Samuel 1:26, David says that Jonathan's love was wonderful and that it surpassed the love of women.

That statement has attracted considerable attention because of the strength of its language. It tells us that David experienced his relationship with Jonathan as extraordinarily significant.

It is important, however, to read the statement within the larger setting of the ancient world. Biblical writers could use intense language to describe covenant loyalty, friendship, family affection, political allegiance, and grief. The language should neither be weakened automatically nor turned into a claim that the text itself does not explicitly make.

CHAPTER 15

What Was the Relationship Between David and Jonathan?

This question has been debated for centuries, although the modern form of the debate is much more recent.

Some interpreters understand David and Jonathan as an example of profound covenant friendship.

Others believe the language allows for a homoerotic interpretation.

The historical evidence does not allow us to state with certainty that they had a sexual relationship.

The biblical text never explicitly says that they did.

At the same time, the language describing their affection is unusually strong.

First Samuel 18:1 describes Jonathan's soul as being bound to David's soul. Jonathan is also said to love David as himself. Their covenant, Jonathan's protection of David, and David's later grief all reinforce the extraordinary nature of the relationship.

Second Samuel 1:26 adds another important piece of evidence. David describes Jonathan's love as wonderful and says that it surpassed the love of women.

These passages provide legitimate grounds for historical discussion. They should not simply be dismissed because the relationship is between two men. At the same time, they should not be presented as proof of a sexual relationship when Scripture does not explicitly provide that information.

The responsible historical approach is therefore neither to declare certainty nor to dismiss the question.

We can say what the text tells us.

  • They loved each other deeply.
  • They made a covenant.
  • They remained loyal to one another.
  • Jonathan protected David at great personal risk.
  • David mourned Jonathan profoundly.
  • The biblical language describing their relationship is unusually intense.

Whether it was sexual is not explicitly answered by Scripture.

That uncertainty is not a weakness in the historical record. It is simply part of the evidence. A careful study can acknowledge what the text clearly says, identify what later interpreters have proposed, and leave unanswered questions where the surviving evidence does not provide a definitive answer.

WHAT DAVID AND JONATHAN ADD TO THE JOURNEY

The story of David and Jonathan demonstrates that the Hebrew Bible is capable of describing profound emotional intimacy between two men without explicitly defining that relationship in modern sexual categories.

The text gives us covenant, love, loyalty, protection, grief, and extraordinary affection. It does not explicitly tell us that David and Jonathan had a sexual relationship.

The historical task is therefore to take the strength of the language seriously while respecting the limits of the evidence.
