ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Bradley Hobbs

Educator and author with a continuing interest in Scripture, thoughtful study, and the questions that shape how people understand the biblical record.

Bradley Hobbs approaches writing as an opportunity to examine meaningful questions with care, clarity, and respect for the reader. His work brings together an interest in biblical interpretation, historical context, and the responsibility to distinguish what a source actually says from the conclusions later readers may draw from it.

A COMMITMENT TO CAREFUL STUDY

Reading with Context and Integrity

In Sodom and Gomorrah, Hobbs takes readers through biblical passages and historical questions that have shaped the city's enduring place in religious thought. The study considers Genesis, the prophetic writings, Israel's legal material, and the New Testament as distinct sources, giving attention to the purpose and setting of each. Rather than treating every reference as though it makes the same argument, the work asks readers to consider the contribution and limits of each passage.

This approach reflects a broader commitment to thoughtful inquiry: evidence should be presented honestly, interpretation should be identified as interpretation, and uncertainty should not be hidden behind claims of certainty. Difficult subjects deserve direct engagement, careful language, and attention to the dignity of those affected by the questions being discussed.

EDUCATION AND WRITING

Learning, Teaching, and Communication

Hobbs's background in education informs his interest in making complex material accessible without removing the distinctions that give the subject meaning. He values clear explanation, organized learning, and writing that invites readers to examine the material rather than simply accept a conclusion.

His writing spans biblical study and broader reflections on human experience. Across these subjects, he seeks to communicate with purpose, preserve the integrity of the material, and encourage readers to think carefully about the ideas before them.

THE AUTHOR'S INVITATION

Consider the Evidence for Yourself

This study is offered as an invitation to read closely, ask honest questions, and weigh the evidence with patience. Readers may arrive with different backgrounds and convictions. The hope is that each will find value in returning to the sources, recognizing the difference between the ancient text and modern interpretation, and allowing careful study to shape a more informed understanding.
